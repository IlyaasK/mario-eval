A reward function for 24-hour RL or agent evaluation has to get every case you already understand right before you trust it on open-ended search. Before running agents, we built 17 calibration patches on Super Mario 64 (spanning retail IDO, GCC -O2, individual Kaze Emanuar techniques, our full-stack Kaze reference ladder, and deliberate reward-hacking traps) and graded every one across public and held-out routes. Full tables live on the calibration page.

Every shortcut fails, and the grader names why

Negative patches cut work in ways a player would notice or that violate evaluation integrity. Every one scores −1.0 across all seven profiles:

  • -ffast-math (GCC -O2 -ffast-math on SM64) is the most natural first flag an agent tries, and fails gameplay/video verification when non-IEEE float contractions drift simulation state.
  • Halving physics substeps (2 quarter-steps instead of 4 in SM64's mario_step.c) is caught immediately on frame 1 by gameplay verification.
  • Muting audio synthesis fails both PLAY=1 active-audio checks and audio SNR (0 dB against the 40 dB floor).
  • Forcing low-poly Mario passes gameplay checks and is caught cleanly by the video gate (exceeding the 0.20% pixel difference limit).
  • Touching mev.c or mev.h is rejected by SHA-256 integrity checks before build.

Whole-frame accepted visual variants

Strict pixel equality against the 1996 ROM would penalize two optimizations that N64 engineers universally consider equal-or-better visual quality: Kaze Emanuar's full-detail Mario LOD fix (always drawing high-poly Mario instead of popping to medium/low-poly meshes at 600/1600 units) and SGI's official G_RM_RA_ZB_OPA_SURF reduced-aliasing opaque render mode (preserving VI edge coverage anti-aliasing while dropping unnecessary per-pixel IM_RD framebuffer reads on opaque surfaces). We precompute those variants from tiny declarative patches, verify at precompute time that they leave gameplay and audio untouched, and check each candidate frame as a whole frame against either the baseline picture or one accepted variant — preventing per-pixel cherry-picking while rewarding genuine N64 rendering upgrades.

Cross-budgets catch unprincipled resource trades

Lookup tables trade RAM for CPU cycles; aggressive unrolling trades I-cache traffic for instruction count. Under the 4 MB retail N64 RAM gate, console and stutter permit full use of the 4 MB RDRAM budget, while the dedicated zero-regression memory profile isolates pure footprint savings such as double-buffering SM64's framebuffers (r5-double-buffer, saving 153.6 KiB of RDRAM).

The direct-mapped cache lottery

Small changes in code or data size can shift symbols across 8 KB D-cache or 16 KB I-cache set boundaries. On Super Mario 64, inspecting sm64.us.map and moving math_util.o adjacent to rendering_graph_node.o in sm64.ld cut VR4300 I-cache RDRAM traffic by over 50% on Bob-omb Battlefield. Reliable N64 optimization requires owning linker layout rather than hoping the compiler gets lucky.
